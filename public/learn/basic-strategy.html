Blackjack basic strategy chart

Basic strategy is the mathematically best way to play every hand when you know nothing but your cards and the dealer's upcard. It cuts the house edge to about half a percent, and it's the foundation every card counter builds on.

This chart is for 6 decks, dealer stands on soft 17, double after split allowed, blackjack pays 3:2, no surrender, the rules the TrueCountLab trainer uses and a very common shoe game. Find your hand in the left column and the dealer's upcard along the top.

How to learn it

The chart looks like a lot, but it follows a few patterns. Learn them, then memorize the exceptions:

  • Dealer 2 to 6 is weak, 7 to ace is strong. Against a weak upcard you stand on stiff hands (13 to 16) and let the dealer bust. Against a strong one you hit them.
  • 12 is the exception among stiffs: stand only against 4, 5 and 6.
  • Double 11 against everything but an ace, 10 against everything but 10 and ace, and 9 against 3 to 6.
  • Always split aces and 8s. Never split 5s or tens. Treat a pair of 5s as a hard 10.
  • Soft hands double against the dealer's weakest cards, and the window widens as the hand gets better: soft 13 and 14 against 5 and 6, soft 15 and 16 against 4 to 6, and soft 17 and 18 against 3 to 6. Soft 18 stands against 2, 7 and 8 and hits against 9, 10 and ace. Soft 19 and up always stand.
  • Never take insurance unless you're counting: with Hi-Lo it's right at a true count of +3 or more.

Practice: the Basic Strategy drill deals random hands and grades every decision instantly. A heatmap shows exactly which cells you miss, so you can focus on them. The full chart is also in the trainer.

Other rules change a few cells. If the dealer hits soft 17, or surrender is offered, a handful of plays differ. The patterns above still hold.
