Index plays: when the count changes the play

Basic strategy assumes a neutral shoe. Once you're counting, you know when the shoe is rich or poor in tens, and for some hands that flips the right decision. Each of these plays has an index: the true count at which you switch.

How to read an index

An index of +4 on "10 vs 10: double" means: at a true count of +4 or higher, double. Below +4, play basic strategy (hit). Negative indices work the same way: "12 vs 5, stand at −2" means stand at −2 or higher, and hit below −2. These indices are for Hi-Lo in a six-deck game where the dealer stands on soft 17, the same game as the basic strategy chart.

Learn these first

A small set of plays does most of the work. Many counters know them as the Illustrious 18, a list ranked by how much each play is worth. Start here:

  1. Insurance at +3. The single most valuable index. At +3 the shoe has enough tens that insurance is a winning bet.
  2. 16 vs 10: stand at 0 or higher. You'll face this hand constantly. Any positive count means stand.
  3. 15 vs 10: stand at +4.
  4. 10 vs 10 and 10 vs ace: double at +4.
  5. 12 vs 2 at +3 and 12 vs 3 at +2: stand.

Tips

  • Master basic strategy first. Index plays are a refinement. Mistakes in basic strategy cost far more than missing an index.
  • Learn in groups. Start with the five above, get them perfect at speed, then add the next few.
  • Use the true count, not the running count. The indices only mean something after dividing by decks remaining.
  • Notice the low-count hits. At negative counts there are fewer tens to bust you, so some stands (like 12 vs 4) turn into hits.

Practice: the Deviations drill shows a hand, a dealer upcard and a true count, and asks whether this is a basic play or an index play. It tracks every index separately so you can see which ones you still miss.
