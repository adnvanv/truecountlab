The true count: how to calculate it

The running count tells you how many extra big cards are left in the shoe. The true count tells you how concentrated they are, and that's what decides your bet and your index plays.

Why you need it

Imagine two shoes, both at a running count of +6. In the first, one deck is left to deal: six extra tens and aces are packed into about 52 cards, a big advantage. In the second, five decks are left, so the same six cards are spread across about 260. The game is barely changed. Dividing by the decks remaining puts both situations on the same scale.

True count = running count ÷ decks remaining, rounded down.

Step 1: estimate the decks remaining

You don't count cards dealt to work this out. You look at the discard tray. Learn what half a deck, one deck, two decks and so on look like as a stack, then:

  1. Estimate how many decks are in the tray, to the nearest half deck.
  2. Subtract that from the number of decks in the game. In a six-deck shoe with 2½ decks in the tray, 3½ decks remain.

Half-deck accuracy is plenty. Counting toward the end of the shoe, when the divisor is small, is where precision matters most, so take extra care there.

Step 2: divide, then round down

Divide the running count by the decks remaining. Then round down to the whole number below (toward minus infinity). So +2.8 becomes +2, and −2.5 becomes −3. Rounding down is the conservative choice: it keeps you from betting big on a count that's only nearly good enough.

Some players truncate toward zero instead, so −2.5 becomes −2. Either works if you're consistent; the TrueCountLab drills always round down, as above.

Shortcuts

  • Halves are just doubling: dividing by 2½ is the same as doubling the count and dividing by 5. With ½ deck left, double the running count.
  • Learn the common divisions cold: the running counts you'll see most often are −10 to +15, and the divisors are only 1 to 6 in halves. A few minutes a day of the drill makes these instant.
  • Only convert when you need it: before placing a bet, and when a hand comes up that has an index play. You don't need the true count after every card.

What the true count means

Each +1 of true count moves the game roughly half a percent toward the player. A typical six-deck game starts around −0.5% for a basic strategy player, so:
