Learn to count cards in blackjack

Card counting is a skill, not a trick. You keep a running tally of the cards you've seen, turn it into a true count, and use that number to decide how much to bet and how to play. This guide takes you through it in five steps, from zero, with a free drill for each one.

The path

  1. Keep the running count (Hi-Lo)

    Low cards are +1, high cards are −1, and 7 to 9 are zero. Learn the values, then count a whole deck down quickly and land on zero.

    Practice: Countdown drill

  2. Convert to the true count

    A +6 means more with one deck left than with five. Divide by the decks remaining, estimated from the discard tray, and round down.

    Practice: Training table (every bet follows the true count)

  3. Play perfect basic strategy

    Counting only pays if every other decision is already right. Memorize the chart for hard totals, soft totals and pairs.

    Practice: Basic Strategy drill

  4. Learn the index plays

    At certain true counts the right play changes: take insurance at +3, stand on 16 against a 10 at 0 or higher, and more.

    Practice: Deviations drill

  5. Bet with the count, at a real table

    Raise your bet when the true count is high, and keep it at the minimum otherwise. Then put it all together: count, convert, bet and play at full speed in a simulated six-deck shoe.

    Practice: the Table · Training Table (shows the answers) · Bankroll calculator

Ready to play? The trainer's home page has a checklist that unlocks once your speed and accuracy are good enough on every drill at the same time. That's a sensible bar to clear before trying it with real money.

How long it takes

Most people can recite the Hi-Lo values after one evening. Getting fast and accurate enough for a casino takes longer: plan on a few weeks of short daily sessions (15 to 20 minutes) to count a deck down in under 30 seconds, convert to the true count without thinking, and play basic strategy without hesitating. The index plays come after that. Short, frequent practice beats long sessions.

Questions

Is card counting illegal?

Counting cards in your head is not a crime in the United States, the UK or most other places. Using a device to help you count is illegal in Nevada and many other jurisdictions. Casinos are private businesses, so they can ask a counter to stop playing blackjack, limit their bets or ask them to leave. This isn't legal advice; check the rules where you play.

Does card counting still work?

Yes, in games with good rules and deep enough penetration (how far into the shoe the dealer deals before shuffling). Continuous shuffling machines, 6:5 blackjack payouts and shallow penetration remove most or all of the advantage, so choosing games matters as much as counting.

Which counting system should I learn?

Hi-Lo. It's a balanced, level-one count: every card is worth +1, 0 or −1, a full deck adds up to zero, and it has the most published index plays. More complex systems gain very little for the extra effort and the extra mistakes.

How much of an edge does it give?

With good basic strategy the house keeps roughly half a percent. Each +1 of true count shifts about half a percent toward the player, so a counter who raises bets at high counts typically plays with an overall edge of around 0.5% to 1.5%. It's a small edge with big swings, which is why bankroll matters. The bankroll calculator shows expected win per hour and risk of ruin for a given bet spread.
